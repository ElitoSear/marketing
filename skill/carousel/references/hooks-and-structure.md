# Hooks, structure and ranking signals

Ranking claims below are community-reported, not official: check anything you rely on against a dated primary source.

## Cover (slide 1)

- About 6 words as the big text, one supporting line. It must work as a grid thumbnail.
- It promises a payoff and opens a loop (a gap the viewer needs closed). A bare topic title fails.
- The hook works by opening a specific gap the viewer wants closed: a stake, a claim, a contrast or an outcome shown while its how is withheld. It must be concrete enough to feel answerable and incomplete enough that only the next slide resolves it. A vague topic opens no gap.
- The picture alone must break the feed's visual rhythm at thumbnail size: it differs from what surrounds it in scale, contrast, subject or density, and needs no words to be noticed.

## Spines by goal

| Goal         | Spine                                                                                         |
| ------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Saves        | Cover, strongest point, one point per slide, recap slide that works as a screenshot, one ask  |
| Shares/sends | Myth vs truth or relatable truth per slide, last slide asks to send it to one specific person |
| Follows      | Before/after or transformation, steps, result restated, follow for the full system            |

- 6 to 10 slides; never pad. Fewer than 4 real points means a single image.
- Best point on slide 2 or 3. One idea per slide, at most about 25 words under a headline.
- The slide after the cover is a second cover: Instagram can re-show a carousel starting from a later slide.
- Number the items when the format is a list so an open loop ("most miss number 4") pays off. A numeral that is part of the content is fine; numbering as decoration ("3/8", "Step 3 of 6" on every slide) is not.
- Handle small on the closing slide, and on every slide once the config's `brand` carries a confirmed handle for the platform (until then the lockup name carries attribution): screenshots travel without you.

## Narrative structures

Each is a different reason to keep swiping; pick by what the content is, then adapt.

| Content | Structure | Engine of the swipe |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A list of resources, tools or tips | Value stack: the cover states an exact count and deliverable, one item per slide, same layout, no filler | A countable promise the reader can check |
| A result with a system behind it | Problem and proof: a result stated as fact, the real problem, the named mechanism, the literal detail, then the receipt | The cover opens a loop the last slide closes |
| Several named techniques on one theme | Hack list: a contrarian cover, why the usual way fails, one named technique per slide, a one-line thesis | Each technique re-earns the swipe alone |
| A strong opinion about a common practice | Callout: a sharp claim, escalating specifics, a fairness pivot that says what you are not attacking, a signed close | Conviction kept fair, so it reads as a position |
| A product or workflow you can show | Demo walkthrough: the finished result, the problem it replaces, the step list, one real screenshot per step, the result | Seeing the whole path early lowers mid-swipe drop-off |

- Name every technique: named things travel and get credited.
- Hacks that restate each other collapse into one slide; the count is a promise, so never pad it.
- Screenshots crop to the action and read at thumbnail size; mockups built from rectangles read as vaporware.

## The cover's hook

A hook is not a line; it is the picture, the words and the claim working apart. The picture stops the thumb, the title opens the loop, and the smaller text anchors the claim for someone who swipes past in a second. They complement each other: a title that captions its own picture wastes one of the three.

Opening moves, to cycle through rather than cluster on one:

- **Curiosity gap**: withhold the noun; it must pay off within the carousel.
- **Bold claim**: a specific, falsifiable statement the slides then substantiate.
- **Confession**: "I was doing this wrong"; needs lived-in detail or it reads fake.
- **Contrast**: two states named or shown in the first beat; the transformation must be honest.
- **Relatable moment**: a hyper-specific situation the viewer recognises; generic ones are invisible.
- **Question**: the exact question the audience types into a search box, in their words.
- **Proof first**: lead with the receipt, the result or the demo.

Take the audience's own vocabulary from their reviews, comments and niche content, not from marketing language, and check that the second slide continues the premise of the cover instead of pivoting to a pitch.

## Signals that matter (reported)

Sends per reach and saves weigh more than likes; comments with real replies help; engagement bait ("comment YES", "tag 3 friends") is downranked. A "comment WORD to get the pack" CTA is common and works for comments, but only use it when a real resource is delivered. Hashtags: a few relevant ones; long blocks read as spam.

## Voice and anti-slop

Scrub AI-sounding vocabulary and rhythm: no "unlock", "elevate", "game-changer", "dive into"; no stacked rule-of-three; no "The result?" reveals; no em dashes. Concrete beats abstract. Never invent a figure, date, name or result: drop the claim rather than decorate it.

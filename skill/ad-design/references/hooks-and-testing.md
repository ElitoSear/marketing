# Hooks, variants and platform text

Platform limits drift: re-check anything you rely on against the platform's current documentation.

## A hook is three parts

The first seconds decide whether the rest of the ad exists. A hook is the picture, the words and the claim working apart:

| Part | Job |
| --- | --- |
| Picture (what is on screen in seconds 0 to 3) | Stops the thumb |
| Spoken or headline line | Opens the loop |
| On-screen text | Anchors the claim for someone watching muted |

They complement each other. A headline that captions its own picture, or a voiceover repeated as a caption, wastes one of the three. A static ad has two parts, picture and headline, and the same rule holds.

## Opening moves

Cycle through them rather than clustering on a favourite.

| Move | Shape | Watch out |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Curiosity gap | Withhold the noun | Must pay off within the ad |
| Bold claim | A specific, falsifiable statement | Needs substantiation on screen |
| Confession | "I was doing this wrong" | Reads fake without lived-in detail |
| Contrast | Two states shown or named in the first beat | The transformation must be honest |
| Relatable moment | A hyper-specific situation | Generic ones are invisible |
| Question | The exact question the buyer types into a search box | Use their words |
| Countdown | A timer or challenge that promises a payoff | The payoff must exist |
| Proof first | Lead with the receipt, the result or the demo | Strongest when the proof speaks alone |

## Variants

Work top-down so hooks do not read like everyone else's:

1. **Segment**: one narrow slice of the audience with a shared situation, taken from real reviews, comments and sales conversations.
2. **Motivation**: the one pain, desire or objection that moves that segment, in their own words.
3. **Format**: the vehicle (comparison, one big fact, review quote, founder message, question and answer, demo, screen recording) chosen before the line is written.
4. **Hook**: the three parts for that cell.

Spread variants across segments and motivations, not across rewordings of one: ten different cells teach more than thirty phrasings of one. Change one thing per test round: the hook picture, or the on-ramp, or the offer.

## The on-ramp

The seconds after the hook (roughly 3 to 15 in video, the supporting line in a static) extend the hook's premise. A hook that promises a cause is followed by the cause, not by the brand story. When a new hook goes onto an existing ad, rewrite the on-ramp to match.

## Reading results

Each stage of the funnel points at a different part:

| Weak stage | Likely cause | Fix |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Stopping (3-second view rate) | The picture | A new opening picture, everything else unchanged |
| Staying (hold rate) | The on-ramp | Rework what follows the hook |
| Clicking | The offer or call to action | Sharpen the promise or the proof |
| Converting | The page does not continue the ad | Fix the page or the claim |

A strong stop with a weak hold is a clickbait picture that attracts the wrong viewers, not a winning hook. Spend production effort in proportion to evidence: a hunch ships as a cheap static or text-on-screen video, and only angles that already showed a signal earn a shoot or a polished video.

## Platform text

Written alongside the design, for the upload form. Over-limit text is truncated without warning.

| Platform | Element | Limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Meta | Primary text | 125 characters visible, 2,200 allowed; front-load the hook |
| Meta | Headline | about 40 characters |
| Meta | Description | about 30 characters |
| LinkedIn | Intro text | about 150 characters, 600 allowed |
| LinkedIn | Headline | about 70 characters |
| TikTok | Ad text | about 80 characters, 100 allowed |
| X | Post text | 280 characters |

## Grounding

Every concept cites the material it came from. No invented claims, statistics or testimonials. Use customer language verbatim over paraphrase, including the niche's own vocabulary from comments and organic posts; take vocabulary and visual conventions from organic content, never a creator's specific creative.
